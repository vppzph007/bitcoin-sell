## Which way should you sell Bitcoin?

There are three practical routes to consider.

### 1. Instant Sell

The instant Sell flow is designed for users who want to convert Bitcoin into cash or another supported asset without managing an order book.

You enter the amount of BTC, select the currency or payment method, and review the quoted amount before confirming. The quote generally includes the platform’s execution price and any applicable spread or service cost in the displayed result.

This is usually the easiest option when:

- You are selling a small or moderate amount.
- You want a quick conversion.
- You do not want to choose a limit price.
- You prefer a guided process in the app.
- You have already connected an eligible payment method.

The trade-off is pricing control. The amount shown in the instant Sell flow may not match the price visible on a spot order book because the quoted rate can include a spread. OKX explains that Express Buy/Sell and Convert can show a single all-in price rather than a separate maker or taker fee. The difference between buying and selling quotes is part of the spread.

Before confirming, compare:

- The BTC amount being sold.
- The currency you will receive.
- The final amount after fees or spread.
- The payment method.
- The quote expiration time, if shown.
- Whether the funds go to your cash balance, bank account, card, or another supported destination.

### 2. Spot trading

Spot trading is more useful when you care about price control or want to sell a larger amount in stages.

Instead of accepting a single quote, you choose a trading pair such as BTC/USD or another available BTC market. You can then place a market order or limit order.

A **market order** attempts to execute immediately against the best available bids. This is convenient, but the final average price can change if the market moves or if your order consumes several levels of the order book.

A **limit order** lets you specify the minimum price at which you are willing to sell. The order may remain open until buyers are available at that price. A limit order is not automatically a maker order, however. If it immediately matches an existing order, it can still be charged at the taker rate. OKX bases the fee on how the order executes, not simply on whether the button says “limit.”

Spot trading is a better fit when:

- You want to set a minimum acceptable price.
- You are comfortable reading a trading screen.
- You want to split a sale into multiple orders.
- You are comparing the order-book price with the instant Sell quote.
- You want to reduce the cost of a larger transaction where execution control matters.

### 3. P2P trading

P2P selling matches you with a buyer who pays through a supported local payment method. The available currencies, payment services, and order types vary by location.

The basic process is:

1. Open P2P trading.
2. Select **Sell**.
3. Choose BTC and the currency you want to receive.
4. Filter offers by amount and payment method.
5. Select a buyer’s offer.
6. Enter the amount of Bitcoin or cash value.
7. Confirm the order.
8. Wait for the buyer’s payment.
9. Check your bank or payment account directly.
10. Release the BTC only after the payment has arrived and the amount is correct.

OKX specifically warns users not to rely on SMS messages or screenshots as proof of payment. Confirm the transaction through your banking app or payment account before releasing the cryptocurrency.

P2P can be useful when a preferred payment method is not available in the instant Sell flow. It also adds another step: you must handle the payment confirmation yourself. That makes it less convenient than an instant sale, especially if you want a completely automated cash-out.

## How much does it cost to sell Bitcoin?

The total cost can come from several places.

### Trading fee

For a spot order, the trading fee is calculated from the amount filled and the fee rate applied to your account. A taker order generally fills immediately against existing orders. A maker order rests on the order book and adds liquidity, although a limit order can execute as taker if it matches immediately.

For example, if you sell $1,000 of BTC at a 0.35% taker fee, the trading fee would be approximately $3.50 before considering any other cost. The actual amount depends on the filled value and the rate shown in your account.

### Spread

The spread is the gap between the price at which buyers are willing to buy and sellers are willing to sell. Instant Buy/Sell quotes may include this difference in the all-in rate.

This is why comparing only the advertised trading fee can give an incomplete picture. A route with a low visible fee may still produce a less favorable final amount if its quoted price is wider than the order-book price.

### Withdrawal fee

If you withdraw Bitcoin to an external wallet, OKX may charge a network withdrawal fee. The amount can change with network conditions and the specific withdrawal route. OKX says deposits do not carry a network fee for the receiving account, while the party withdrawing crypto pays the withdrawal fee. The current amount should be checked on the withdrawal screen before confirming.

If you sell BTC for cash and withdraw fiat, the cost may instead depend on the payment method, bank, card provider, or local settlement route.

### Bank or payment-provider charges

Your bank, card issuer, or payment provider may apply its own charge. That cost is separate from OKX’s trading fee. A payment method may also have limits, processing times, or eligibility requirements.

## How to sell Bitcoin using a limit order

A limit order is useful when you have a minimum price in mind.

1. Open the BTC spot market.
2. Select **Limit**.
3. Enter the price at which you want to sell.
4. Enter the BTC quantity.
5. Review the estimated proceeds and fee.
6. Submit the order.
7. Monitor its status in open orders.
8. Cancel or adjust it if market conditions change.

A limit order may remain open indefinitely, depending on the platform’s rules and the order settings. It is not guaranteed to fill. If the market never reaches your selected price, no sale occurs.

The main advantage is control. The main cost is timing: Bitcoin can move away from your limit price while the order waits.

## Should you sell BTC into USD, USDT, or another asset?

That depends on what you want to do next.

### Sell into USD

This is the clearest option if you want to withdraw cash or record a fiat sale. It makes the proceeds easier to compare with your original purchase cost and bank balance.

### Sell into a stablecoin

Selling BTC into a stablecoin can reduce Bitcoin price exposure without immediately moving funds to a bank. However, it is still a crypto-to-crypto disposal for tax purposes in many jurisdictions, including the United States. The conversion also leaves you exposed to the stablecoin’s specific risks and to any later withdrawal or conversion costs.

### Sell into another cryptocurrency

Swapping BTC for another cryptocurrency is still a disposal event for tax purposes in the United States, according to OKX’s U.S. tax guide. The fact that no dollars reach your bank account does not automatically make the transaction tax-free.

Choose the destination based on your actual goal. Converting BTC into another token is not the same as cashing out.

## Bitcoin taxes in the United States

In the United States, cryptocurrency is generally treated as property for tax purposes. Selling Bitcoin for dollars, exchanging it for another cryptocurrency, or using it to pay for goods can create a taxable event.

The gain or loss is generally calculated by comparing:

- The sale or exchange value.
- Your cost basis.
- Relevant transaction fees.
- How long you held the asset.

A sale above your cost basis may create a capital gain. A sale below your cost basis may create a capital loss. Holding period can affect whether the gain is treated as short-term or long-term.

Keep records of:

- The date and time of the sale.
- BTC quantity sold.
- Proceeds received.
- Cost basis.
- Trading fees.
- Transfer fees.
- Wallet transactions.
- Any previous purchases connected to the BTC.

OKX’s own U.S. tax material states that selling or swapping crypto can create a taxable event, but the platform’s educational content is not a substitute for tax advice. If the transaction is substantial or your records are complex, use a qualified tax professional.

## Common mistakes when selling Bitcoin

### Selling without checking the final quote

The headline BTC price is not the same as the amount you receive. Review the final proceeds after spread, trading fee, and any payment cost.

### Using a market order for a large sale

A large market order can fill at several prices. A limit order or staged sale may provide more control.

### Releasing P2P Bitcoin before payment arrives

Never release BTC because a buyer sends a screenshot or claims that payment has been made. Confirm the money in your own account first.

### Forgetting the withdrawal network

When moving remaining crypto off the exchange, confirm the network and address. A network mismatch can create a serious recovery problem.

### Assuming the 20% referral rebate covers every cost

A trading-fee rebate is not the same as a full-cost rebate. It may apply only to eligible trading fees and may not cover spread, withdrawal charges, bank charges, or network fees. Confirm the terms attached to **CASH20** inside your account.

### Ignoring regional availability

OKX services and payment methods differ by jurisdiction. The same Sell button, currency, or cash-out route may not be available in every country or state.
